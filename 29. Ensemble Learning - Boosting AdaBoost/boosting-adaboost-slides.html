Ensemble Learning · Boosting & AdaBoost

Stop building trees
at the same time. Build them
one mistake at a time.

Random Forest built hundreds of trees independently and averaged them. Today: a completely different philosophy — build trees one after another, each one obsessed with fixing exactly what the last one got wrong. That's Boosting, and AdaBoost is where it started.

Recap · Bagging, One More Time

Bootstrap + Aggregate = independent trees

Random Forest: sample rows (and columns) with replacement, build many trees independently and in parallel, then average their votes. Every tree is trained without any knowledge of what the others got right or wrong.

A single Decision Tree is highly sensitive to its training data — its exact structure is one specific perspective on the data. Bagging's fix: build many different perspectives, independently, and let them vote.
The Analogy · Two Research Labs

Independent projects, or learning from the last mistake?

Two labs solve "how do we do better research?" in opposite ways.

🧪

Lab A — Same Fields, Independent Projects

Researcher 1, 2, 3… each run their own independent project. New juniors get independent projects too. They span multiple application ideas, multiple perspectives. This is Bagging.

🔁

Lab B — One Deep Thread, Sequentially

Research 1 → Research 2 → Project 2, building on what came before. Juniors go deep by learning from the mistakes of seniors. Fewer directions, but each one corrects the last. This is Boosting.

Boosting, Defined

Several weak models,
built sequentially, become one strong model

Weak Model 1 → Weak Model 2 (fixes Weak Model 1's mistakes) → Weak Model 3 (fixes what's still wrong) → … Each new model exists specifically to correct the previous ensemble's errors — the opposite instinct from Bagging's "build independently and average."

Weak Learners · What AdaBoost Actually Builds

An army of stumps

Stump
A Decision Tree with exactly one split — one column, one question, two leaves. Barely better than a coin flip on its own. That weakness is deliberate: AdaBoost's strength comes from combining many of them cleverly, not from any single one being smart.

Every column gets its own candidate stump. Whichever column's stump has the highest Information Gain becomes the first weak learner.

Amount of Say · How Much Does This Stump's Vote Count?

α = ½ ln( (1−error) / error )

Every stump earns a weight in the final vote based on how accurate it was — not just "trust everyone equally." For Stump 1 (error = 0.20):

Notice: at error = 0.5 (a coin flip), α is exactly 0 — that stump gets zero say. Below 0.5, α is positive. Above 0.5 — a stump that's wrong more often than right — α goes negative: its vote gets inverted, because being reliably wrong is just as useful as being reliably right.

The Final Prediction

final(x) = sign( Σ αₜ hₜ(x) )

Encode Approve as +1 and Reject as −1. A new applicant (#6) is run through all three stumps; each vote gets scaled by that stump's own α before summing.

A Very Close Call

+0.693 + 0.549 − 1.199 = +0.043

Positive, barely — the final prediction is Approve. Even though Stump 3 individually had the highest confidence (highest α), it's outvoted by the combined weight of Stumps 1 and 2. No single stump ever has the final word — the sum does.

Rapid Fire

Seven questions, answered

🏷️

Why "Adaptive"?

Misclassified samples get more weight — the algorithm adapts to its own mistakes, round by round.

🌱

What's the weak learner?

A stump — a Decision Tree with a single split.

🌳

Can it use deeper trees?

Yes — but it causes overfitting. Stumps are the standard choice on purpose.

📈

How does it re-focus each round?

Exponential increase for wrong rows, exponential decrease for right ones.

🗳️

How's the final prediction made?

sign(Σ αₜ hₜ(x)) — every stump's vote, weighted by its own amount of say.

⚠️

Sensitive to outliers?

Yes. An outlier gets misclassified repeatedly, its weight balloons, and later stumps can over-focus on it.

📉

Can it overfit?

Yes — enough rounds chasing enough noisy points will eventually memorize them.
