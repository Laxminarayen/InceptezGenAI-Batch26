Today's Problem: Retail Customer Segmentation

A shopping mall has Age, Annual Income, and Spending Score for its customers, and wants personalized marketing — different campaigns for different kinds of shoppers. There is no "Segment" column to learn from.

How do I annotate this data?

  1. Manually sit and annotate — a human reads every row and assigns a group by eye. Doesn't scale.
  2. Use a grouping algorithm — find the buckets automatically from the numbers alone, with no pre-existing labels. This is Clustering.
The goal, stated precisely: no pre-existing labels exist, and the algorithm must create groupings/patterns from the data automatically — "natural groups of customers," discovered rather than defined in advance.

K-Means, the Algorithm

  1. Choose K — the number of clusters to look for.
  2. Initialize K centroids randomly — K random starting points in the feature space.
  3. Assign every point to its nearest centroid — measured by Euclidean distance: d = √((x₂−x₁)² + (y₂−y₁)²).
  4. Update each centroid to the mean of its current members, then repeat step 3. Stop when centroids no longer move, or a maximum number of iterations is reached.

Worked Example: 4 Customers, K = 2

Four customers, (Income, Spending): C1 = (25, 80), C2 = (90, 15), C3 = (85, 88), C4 = (30, 20). Initialize 2 centroids at C1 and C4.

Update step

New Centroid A = mean(C1, C3) = ( (25+85)/2, (80+88)/2 ) = (55.0, 84.0) New Centroid B = mean(C2, C4) = ( (90+30)/2, (15+20)/2 ) = (60.0, 17.5). The centroids move toward their members; re-running the assignment step could now reshuffle who belongs where. K-Means repeats assign → update until nothing changes.

Choosing K

There's no accuracy score to tune K against — no ground truth exists for "correct" clusters. In practice, K is chosen by trial and error across a reasonable range (commonly K = 3 to 12), watching how inertia (sum of squared distances from every point to its own centroid) drops as K grows — the "elbow," where that drop flattens out, is a common rule of thumb.
