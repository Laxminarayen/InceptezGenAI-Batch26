Transfer Learning & Deployment (CNNs, Part 3)

Training a deep CNN from scratch needs huge data and days of compute. Transfer learning reuses a model already trained on a giant dataset and adapts it to your task in minutes. We cover pre-trained models, feature extraction vs fine-tuning, the full overfitting toolbox, and deploying a lightweight MobileNet → ONNX → EC2 classifier.

Part I: Transfer Learning

Transfer learning = reusing knowledge learned by a model on one task to help solve another task.

Interview Q — can the tasks be unrelated domains? Can I use a model trained on X-ray data for natural images? No! The patterns aren't useful across unrelated domains. Transfer works when the source and target share visual structure.

1. The pre-trained model

A pre-trained model has its weights & biases already learned from a previous, large training task — classically the ImageNet Challenge. Famous backbones: AlexNet, ResNet, MobileNet.

  • Load pre-trained W & B's — the trained filters of the CNN blocks and FFNN (generic edge/shape detectors), instead of random weights.
  • Freeze the backbone → its weights & biases become FROZEN (not updated).
  • Train a fully-connected head → only those W's & B's update → the model learns to classify your classes.

The pre-trained blocks transfer the learned knowledge; the head (e.g. KNN, Logistic Regression, or a small NN) is replaced to match your number of classes (the ImageNet head had ~1000 classes).

What have we seen so far?

Where we use a pre-trained model without altering the existing learnt W's & B's, but transferring the learning. That's feature extraction / transfer learning.

Part II: What if I want to alter the W's & B's for my dataset too?

Then you fine-tune: unfreeze some layers and train a little more to adapt them to your data.

Initially = 1000 epochs over 3 days  →  Now = 5–10 epochs in 4–5 hours Starting from pre-trained weights, you need far fewer epochs — this is fine-tuning.

Four keywords (keep them separate)

KeywordMeaning
Pre-trainingLearn a reusable knowledge on a big dataset.
Feature extractionFreeze + pass data through the backbone to get features.
Transfer learningFreeze the feature extractor + train the task heads.
Fine-tuningAdapt pre-trained weights & biases for our data.

Part III: Overfitting in Neural Networks

Overfitting = good training performance + poor unseen performance The model memorizes the training set instead of learning general patterns.

Why do NNs overfit? They are data-hungry:

  1. Too little training data.
  2. Training for too many epochs.
  3. Training data does not represent real-life patterns.

Six ways to fight overfitting

  1. Add more data — and for images, data augmentation: horizontal flip, zoom, synthetic oversampling (make new training images from the ones you have).
  2. Dropout — e.g. Dropout(0.2) disables 20% of neurons during training. It reduces reliance on particular neurons & activations, giving a more general, robust model (a deliberate, intentional "under-learning").
  3. Early stopping — stop training when validation performance stops improving (see below).
  4. Reduce model complexity — a 151-layer network on only 500 rows is overkill; use a smaller model.
  5. Transfer learning — general training on other data + simple task fitting on your data.
  6. L2 regularization — penalize the loss with a weight term.

Early stop training when validation performance stops improving — the generalizing model is the one you want, not the memorizing one.

L2 regularization

Total loss = (1/n)·Σ(SE) + λ·Σw² Add a squared penalty on the weights (λ very small). "Don't focus so much on the features to drive the loss to zero (that chases uncertainty) — focus on general patterns."
Interview Q — will there be overfitting in LLMs? Yes, there will be overfitting in LLMs — that is exactly why fine-tuning must be done carefully.

Bonus: Translation Equivariance

If I shift the input, the output shifts by the exact same amount. This is the property that makes convolution natural for images — a feature detected at one location is detected the same way when it moves.

Part IV: Practical — CIFAR-10 → ONNX → EC2

We keep the model light (lower parameter count, smaller storage, fewer dependencies — Dockerization also helps ship the whole thing). We fine-tune MobileNetV3-Small (ImageNet-pretrained) on CIFAR-10 (10 classes), export to ONNX, and serve it with ONNX Runtime on a CPU-only Ubuntu EC2 micro instance — no PyTorch or GPU in production.

# load pre-trained backbone, freeze it, swap the head
model = mobilenet_v3_small(weights=MobileNet_V3_Small_Weights.DEFAULT)
for p in model.parameters():
    p.requires_grad = False                      # freeze backbone
n = model.classifier[-1].in_features
model.classifier[-1] = nn.Linear(n, 10)          # new 10-class head
optimizer = torch.optim.AdamW(model.classifier[-1].parameters(), lr=1e-3)
# ... train a few epochs, keep best val accuracy, then:
torch.onnx.export(model, example, "artifacts/model.onnx", opset_version=17)
