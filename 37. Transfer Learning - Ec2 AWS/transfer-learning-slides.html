Part I · What Is Transfer Learning?

Reuse knowledge from one task to help another

Transfer learning = reusing the knowledge a model learned on one task to help solve another. Instead of learning edges, corners and textures all over again, you inherit them.

pre-trained features + your small dataset → a good model, fastThe low-level visual building blocks are shared across almost all image tasks.
🧪 Interview Q: can the tasks be unrelated domains — e.g. use a model trained on X-ray data for natural images?
Answer: No — the patterns aren't useful across unrelated domains. Transfer works when the source and target share visual structure. The closer the domains, the better the transfer.
Part II · The Pre-trained Model

Weights & biases already learned

A pre-trained model has its weights & biases already learned from a previous, large training task — classically the ImageNet Challenge (~1000 classes, millions of images).

🏗️

AlexNet

The 2012 breakthrough.

🔗

ResNet

Very deep, residual blocks.

📱

MobileNet

Lightweight — our choice for EC2.

Load pre-trained W & B's
You download the trained filters of the CNN blocks and the FFNN — the generic edge/shape detectors — instead of random initial weights.
The recipe: keep the pre-trained backbone (feature extractor), swap in a fresh head for your number of classes, and train.
Four Keywords You Must Separate

Pre-training · Feature extraction · Transfer learning · Fine-tuning

①

Pre-training

Learn a reusable knowledge on a big dataset (someone already did this for ImageNet models).

②

Feature extraction

Freeze the backbone and pass your data through it to get features — the backbone doesn't change.

③

Transfer learning

Freeze the feature extractor + train the task head on your classes.

④

Fine-tuning

Adapt the pre-trained W's & B's to your data by unfreezing some layers and training a little more.

Then vs now: initially we trained 1000 epochs over 3 days. With a pre-trained backbone: 5–10 epochs in 4–5 hours.

Practical · CIFAR-10 with MobileNetV3-Small

Freeze the backbone, swap the head

Load ImageNet-pretrained MobileNetV3-Small, freeze every parameter, then replace the final classifier with a fresh Linear(…, 10) for the 10 CIFAR-10 classes. Only the head trains.

weights = MobileNet_V3_Small_Weights.DEFAULT
model = mobilenet_v3_small(weights=weights)

# freeze the whole backbone — no retraining
for p in model.parameters():
    p.requires_grad = False

# swap the head for 10 classes
n = model.classifier[-1].in_features
model.classifier[-1] = nn.Linear(n, 10)

optimizer = torch.optim.AdamW(
    model.classifier[-1].parameters(), lr=1e-3)
Part III · Overfitting in Neural Networks

Great on training, poor on unseen data

Overfitting = good training performance + poor unseen performanceWhy NNs overfit — they're data-hungry: (1) too little training data, (2) training for too many epochs, (3) training data doesn't represent real life.
Interview Q: will there be overfitting in LLMs? Yes — that's exactly why fine-tuning must be done carefully.
Six Ways to Fight Overfitting

The regularization toolbox

➕

1 · More data

Incl. image augmentation — horizontal flip, zoom, synthetic oversampling.

🎲

2 · Dropout

Dropout(0.2) disables 20% of neurons — less reliance on any one, more robust.

⏹️

3 · Early stopping

Stop when validation accuracy stops improving (don't wait for all 1000 epochs).

🪶

4 · Reduce complexity

A 151-layer net on 500 rows is overkill — shrink the model.

♻️

5 · Transfer learning

General training elsewhere + simple fitting on your data.

⚖️

6 · L2 regularization

Add λ·Σw² to the loss — penalize big weights, favour general patterns.

Early Stopping, Concretely

Stop before it starts to memorize

When train accuracy keeps climbing but validation stalls or dips, the model is starting to memorize the training set.
✓ Early stop training when validation performance stops improving — the generalizing model is the one you want, not the memorizing one.
Part IV · Ship It · Lightweight Deployment

MobileNet → ONNX → EC2

Keep the model light: fewer parameters, smaller storage, fewer dependencies. Export to ONNX and serve it with ONNX Runtime on a CPU-only Ubuntu EC2 micro instance — no PyTorch or GPU needed in production.

🧠

Train (Colab)

MobileNetV3-Small on CIFAR-10 → model.onnx + classes.json.

🌐

Serve (Flask)

ONNX Runtime loads the model; upload an image → top-3 classes.

☁️

Deploy (EC2)

Copy artifacts, gunicorn, open the port. Runs on 1 GiB RAM.

# export to ONNX for a PyTorch-free server
torch.onnx.export(
    model, example, "artifacts/model.onnx",
    input_names=["images"],
    output_names=["logits"],
    opset_version=17)

# EC2: pip install onnxruntime (no torch!)

Recap

Stand on giant shoulders

Transfer learning reuses a pre-trained model's weights (ImageNet → AlexNet/ResNet/MobileNet). Feature extraction freezes the backbone and trains a new head; fine-tuning unfreezes some layers to adapt. Fight overfitting with more data/augmentation, dropout, early stopping, smaller models, transfer learning, and L2. Then ship light — MobileNet → ONNX → a CPU EC2 micro.
