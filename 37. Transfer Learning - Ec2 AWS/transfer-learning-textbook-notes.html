1.What Is Transfer Learning?

Transfer learning = reusing the knowledge a model learned on one task to help solve another. The low-level visual features (edges, textures, shapes) are shared across almost all image tasks, so you inherit them instead of re-learning them.

Interview Q — can the tasks be unrelated domains? Can a model trained on X-ray data be used for natural images? No — the patterns aren't useful across unrelated domains. Transfer works when source and target share visual structure; the closer the domains, the better.

2.Pre-trained Models

A pre-trained model has its weights & biases already learned from a previous, large training task — classically the ImageNet Challenge (~1000 classes, millions of images). Famous backbones:

ModelNote
AlexNetThe 2012 breakthrough that started the deep-learning vision era.
ResNetVery deep via residual (skip) connections.
MobileNetLightweight, designed for phones/edge — our choice for a small EC2 box.

You load the pre-trained W & B's — the trained filters of the CNN blocks and the dense layers — rather than starting from random weights.

3.Freeze the Backbone, Train the Head

The standard recipe: keep the pre-trained backbone (feature extractor) as-is, replace the head with a fresh classifier for your number of classes, and train only the head.

4.Four Keywords (Keep Them Separate)

KeywordMeaning
Pre-trainingLearn a reusable knowledge on a big dataset (done once, by whoever trained the ImageNet model).
Feature extractionFreeze the backbone and pass your data through it to obtain features.
Transfer learningFreeze the feature extractor + train the task head on your classes.
Fine-tuningAdapt the pre-trained weights & biases to your data (unfreeze some layers, train more).

6.Overfitting in Neural Networks

Overfitting = good training performance + poor unseen performanceThe model memorizes the training set instead of learning general patterns.

Why NNs overfit — they are data-hungry: (1) too little training data, (2) training for too many epochs, (3) training data doesn't represent real-life patterns.

7.Six Remedies

  1. Add more data — including image augmentation: horizontal flip, zoom, synthetic oversampling (generate new training images from existing ones).
  2. Dropout — e.g. Dropout(0.2) disables 20% of neurons each step, reducing reliance on any particular neuron/activation → a more general, robust model (deliberate "under-learning").
  3. Early stopping — stop when validation performance stops improving.
  4. Reduce model complexity — don't put a 151-layer net on 500 rows; use a smaller model.
  5. Transfer learning — general training elsewhere + simple task fitting on your data.
  6. L2 regularization — penalize large weights in the loss.

Total loss = (1/n)·Σ(SE) + λ·Σw²L2 adds a squared penalty on the weights (λ very small). Intuition: don't chase every feature to drive the loss to zero (that fits noise/uncertainty) — favour general patterns.
Interview Q — will there be overfitting in LLMs? Yes — which is exactly why fine-tuning a large model must be done carefully (small learning rates, few epochs, held-out eval).

Bonus: translation equivariance

If you shift the input, the output shifts by the same amount. This property is why convolution suits images — a feature is detected the same way wherever it appears.

9.Deployment: MobileNet → ONNX → EC2

Keep the model light: lower parameter count, smaller storage, fewer dependencies (Dockerization also helps ship everything together). We export the trained model to ONNX and serve it with ONNX Runtime on a CPU-only Ubuntu EC2 micro instance — no PyTorch or GPU in production.

# training machine: export to ONNX
torch.onnx.export(model, example, "artifacts/model.onnx",
                  input_names=["images"], output_names=["logits"],
                  opset_version=17)

# EC2 (CPU only): onnxruntime, Pillow, Flask — NO torch, NO GPU
session = ort.InferenceSession("artifacts/model.onnx",
                               providers=["CPUExecutionProvider"])

10.Summary

The whole idea, in one paragraph

Transfer learning reuses a model pre-trained on a huge dataset (ImageNet → AlexNet / ResNet / MobileNet) instead of training from scratch — but only across related domains. You load the pre-trained weights, freeze the backbone, and train a new head for your classes (feature extraction / transfer learning); unfreezing some layers to adapt them is fine-tuning, which now takes 5–10 epochs instead of 1000. Because NNs are data-hungry and overfit (great on train, poor on unseen), you reach for the toolbox: more data & augmentation, dropout, early stopping, smaller models, transfer learning, and L2. Finally you ship light: export MobileNet to ONNX and serve it with ONNX Runtime on a CPU-only EC2 micro. Next: NLP → RNNs → Attention & Transformers.
