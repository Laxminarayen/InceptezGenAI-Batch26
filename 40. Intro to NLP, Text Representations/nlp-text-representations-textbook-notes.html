Intro to NLP & Text Representations

How do you turn text into numbers a model can learn from? This chapter walks the ladder of text representations — Bag of Words, Count Vectorizer, TF-IDF, n-grams — and why each falls short, then introduces Word2Vec: learned word embeddings (CBOW & Skip-gram) that finally capture meaning as geometry.

1.What Is NLP?

NLP = Natural Language + Processing — running some kind of process over human language. A natural language (Malayalam, English) differs from a programming language (Python): it is organically developed, evolved for a human community, and passed through generations, changing over time. In the big picture, AI ⊃ ML ⊃ Deep Learning, and NLP spans across all of them; today we point deep learning at language.

2.Properties of Language

PropertyMeaning
PhonologySound units (phonemes) — e.g. the "dh" sound in father.
SyntaxWord order & sentence structure — the sequentially ordered placement of words (S-V-O, S-V-P).
LexiconThe linguistic collection of words.
Multiple nested dependencies — the core challenge. "Lammi likes to eat Italian food. He is very fond of this cuisine." To understand "He" (→ Lammi) and "this cuisine" (→ Italian food) you must carry context across sentences. Language is not a set of independent words — meaning depends on what came before.

3.The Core Problem: Text → Numbers

An image is already numeric — an m × n × 3 grid of pixel values — so a network can read it directly. Text has no such form. "The movie is good" must be turned into numbers before any model (Logistic Regression, KNN, a neural net) can use it. The rest of this chapter is a sequence of representations, each fixing a flaw of the previous one.

4.Bag of Words (One-Hot)

Lowercase the text and remove stop words (highly frequent words with little standalone meaning: the, is, a, of). Take the unique content words as columns and each sentence as a row; put 1 if the word appears, else 0. This is the document-term matrix.

Three disadvantages

  1. Word order / meaning lost — "the whole experience is a bad experience" has the same bag whatever the order; sequence is gone.
  2. Frequency ignored — 0/1 cannot distinguish a word used once from ten times. Needs dynamic weightage.
  3. No semantic meaning — relationships like KING − MAN + WOMAN = QUEEN (royalty, rich…) are invisible.

5.Count Vectorizer (Term Frequency)

Fixes disadvantage #2: each cell becomes the count of the word in the sentence rather than 0/1. Now "waste waste" scores 2, capturing emphasis. But a word that appears a lot across the whole document set (like "movie" in a set of movie reviews) is still treated as important when it isn't distinctive — the next step handles that.

6.TF-IDF

Term Frequency × Inverse Document Frequency gives each word a dynamic weight: high where the word is distinctive, low where it is everywhere.

TF = (word count) ÷ (words in the sentence)
IDF = log( total documents ÷ documents containing the word )
TF-IDF = TF × IDF A word in every document → IDF = log(1) = 0 → weight vanishes, like an automatic stop word.

7.n-grams & the Remaining Gap

n-grams capture a little local order: n=1 uses single words (movie, time, good); n=2 uses adjacent pairs ("movie good", "time wasted"). Useful, but it does not solve the deeper problem.

The shared flaw. One-hot, Count Vector and TF-IDF all have no paired association of meaning — no semantics — and they depend on manual feature engineering. "good" and "great" are as unrelated as "good" and "aardvark". We need a representation that learns meaning.

8.Word2Vec: Learned Embeddings

Instead of assigning numbers by hand, give a neural network sentences and a prediction task involving nearby words. Solving "which word belongs here?" forces the network to learn useful word vectors as a by-product — representation learning.

Learn word predictions ⟹ learn word vectorsA word's ID is arbitrary (cat = 1, dog = 2). Word ID ≠ word meaning — the learned vector carries the meaning.

Context window — the neighbouring words around a target. In "The cat [drinks] fresh milk" the target is drinks and the context is the, cat, fresh, milk.

9.CBOW, Skip-gram & the Trick

Where the vector comes from. Word2Vec is a shallow neural network. Its output (classification) layer has one neuron per vocabulary word (~70,000 = vocab size). After training we don't want the prediction — we remove the classification head and keep the hidden layer: the learned hidden weights for each word are its embedding.

Semantic geometry — the payoff

Because similar words appear in similar contexts, their learned vectors land near each other. Meaning becomes geometry:

  • Clusters — cat and dog sit together; car and bus form their own neighbourhood.
  • Vector arithmetic — KING − MAN + WOMAN ≈ QUEEN; relationships are directions in the space.
  • Usage shapes position — a verb like "consume" pulls drank toward beverage contexts.
This is the semantic meaning BoW and TF-IDF never had — learned automatically from text, with no hand-built features.

10.Summary

The whole idea, in one paragraph

NLP turns text into numbers. The simplest way, Bag of Words, marks whether each vocabulary word is present (0/1) after removing stop words — but it loses order, ignores frequency and has no semantics. Count Vectorizer adds frequency; TF-IDF weights each word by how distinctive it is (a word in every document gets weight 0); n-grams add a little local order. Yet all three still lack meaning and lean on feature engineering. Word2Vec fixes this by learning the representation: a shallow network predicts a word from its neighbours (CBOW) or the neighbours from a word (Skip-gram); after training, its hidden layer gives each word a dense ~128-dim embedding where similar words cluster and relationships become arithmetic.
